Final Year Project · 2025–2026

Beyond the Plug.

Designing Practical and Sustainable EV Charging Experiences.

A user-centred exploration of how digital design can make public EV charging easier to understand, more reliable and better suited to the everyday needs of drivers.

Project

Final Year Project

Role

UX/UI Designer & Researcher

Tools

Figma · Miro · Canva · HTML/CSS/JavaScript

Methods

Surveys · Interviews · Comparative Analysis · Prototyping · User Testing

01

Overview

Looking beyond the act of charging.

EV charging is not only a technical interaction. Drivers need to find chargers, assess reliability, plan routes, wait, pay and feel confident about where they are stopping.

As an EV driver, I had experienced many of these frustrations first-hand, including inconsistent charging networks, unreliable station information and the challenge of finding suitable places to stop.

Beyond the Plug became an opportunity to explore these wider experiences through user research, interaction design and iterative prototyping.

Research Question

How can Interaction Design principles be applied to improve the user experience of EV charging?
02

The Problem

Charging is part of a much bigger journey.

Early research highlighted recurring issues that extend beyond simply locating a charger.

01

Reliability

Drivers need confidence that charging information is accurate before committing to a stop.

02

Fragmentation

Different networks and applications can make the charging experience feel disconnected.

03

Route Planning

Charging needs to fit naturally into the driver's wider journey rather than being treated separately.

04

Safety

Lighting, location and surroundings can influence how comfortable a driver feels while charging.

05

Accessibility

Charging information should support a wider range of users and physical accessibility requirements.

06

Personalisation

Drivers value different things when choosing where to stop, from amenities to preferred networks.

03

Research

Understanding the people behind the journey.

I used a mixed-method research approach combining a quantitative survey, qualitative interviews and analysis of existing charging and navigation applications.

27 Survey responses
5 User interviews
7 Apps reviewed
3.30/5 Average safety rating

The survey used convenience sampling through personal and social networks. I therefore treated the findings as exploratory and directional rather than representative of all EV drivers.

Comparative Analysis

Learning from the existing ecosystem.

I reviewed Ecar Connect, EVBox Everon, Shell Recharge, EasyGo, PlugShare, Waze and Google Maps to understand existing interaction patterns and identify gaps.

Existing products provided useful charging information, mapping and community features, but the wider experience often remained fragmented across different services.

01

Improve trust in charging information.

02

Bring route planning and charging together.

03

Surface safety and accessibility information.

04

Allow drivers to filter around their priorities.

05

Make sustainability information more visible.

Synthesising the Research

From conversations to design opportunities.

04

Defining the Experience

Turning research into user needs.

Research findings were developed into personas, scenarios, storyboards and user flows to connect individual frustrations with concrete design decisions.

05

From Ideas to Interface

Explore. Structure. Refine.

01 / Explore

Sketching the possibilities.

Early sketches allowed me to quickly explore interface layouts, navigation patterns, features and visual directions before committing to a digital solution.

02 / Structure

Building the first experience.

Low-fidelity prototyping focused on hierarchy, navigation and task flow without the distraction of detailed visual styling.

03 / Refine

Moving into Figma.

The medium-fidelity prototype developed the structure into a more complete interface and provided a foundation for user testing and further iteration.

06

Testing & Iteration

Designing through feedback.

Prototype testing helped identify navigation issues, missing screens and areas where the interface required greater clarity.

01

Observe

Participants completed tasks while I observed navigation behaviour, hesitation and unexpected interactions.

02

Identify

Testing highlighted inefficient navigation, missing states and screens that required additional explanation.

03

Iterate

Feedback informed revisions to the user flow, navigation and supporting interface screens before the final prototype.

07 · Visual Design

A visual system shaped around clarity and energy.

High-Fidelity Prototyping

Designed across mobile and desktop.

The final prototype was developed across both mobile and desktop interfaces, adapting the same visual system, features and interaction patterns to suit different screen sizes and contexts.

08

Final Experience

Bringing the charging journey together.

The final design was developed across both mobile and desktop experiences, creating a consistent system for charger discovery, route planning, contextual information and charging management across different screen sizes.

01 / Discover

Find the right place to stop.

Charger discovery combines mapping with contextual information so drivers can evaluate a location before committing to it.

02 / Evaluate

Make a more informed choice.

Charger information brings relevant station details together to support more confident decision-making.

03 / Plan

Build charging into the journey.

Route planning integrates charging stops into the wider trip and allows users to filter locations around their preferences.

04 / Charge

Keep the experience clear from start to finish.

The charging flow supports setup, progress and completion using a consistent interface and clear feedback.

Cross-Platform Design

Designed for mobile and desktop.

Beyond the Plug was developed across both mobile and desktop prototypes. While the same visual language, features and interaction principles were maintained across both versions, layouts were adapted to make effective use of each screen size.

Desktop Prototype

Extending the experience across screen sizes.

The desktop version demonstrates how the interface and design system can scale beyond mobile while retaining consistent navigation, visual hierarchy and interaction patterns.

A mobile version was also developed, adapting the same core experience for a smaller, journey-focused interface.

09 · Outcome & Reflection

More than a charger finder.

Beyond the Plug developed into an exploration of the entire charging experience — before, during and after a driver reaches a station.

The project strengthened my understanding of how research can be translated into interface decisions and how iterative testing can expose issues that are difficult to identify during design alone.

It also encouraged me to consider EV charging as a wider service experience involving trust, safety, accessibility, sustainability and the driver's surrounding environment.
